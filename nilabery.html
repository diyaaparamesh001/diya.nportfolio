Design Experiment

AI makes the designs.
Who makes the decisions?

A website was generated. The design questions were just beginning.

AI gave me a great starting point for the Nilabery Studios website, but I wasn’t entirely convinced by the result. The more I explored the website, the more questions I had about its design.

About the brand

Nilabery Studios is a creative studio working across branding, advertising, and filmmaking. They wanted a website that reflected their maximalist outlook, showcased their best work and services, and drew people in through bold visuals and unexpected storytelling.

The thinking before the prompt

Without enough context, AI can fall back on familiar design patterns, producing websites that feel generic rather than distinctive. I wanted to give Claude a clearer direction by defining the visual identity, typography, content, and personality I had in mind.

What I included in the prompt

    Creative, funky, and maximalist.

  • 02

Now, let’s take each section one by one and explore the thinking behind my design decisions.

The Hero Page

I wanted the hero section to do more than introduce the studio. The title and subtext needed to capture Nilabery’s personality and make visitors feel what the brand was all about.

The Logo

While AI tried to capture Nilabery’s personality, the logo felt cluttered and visually inconsistent. I simplified it, placing the “N” inside the blueberry to create a doorway into the studio’s creative world.

The Services Page

I applied key UX principles to redesign this section.

01

Grouping & Consistency

Related services are presented within clearly defined categories, using a consistent card layout to make the information easier to scan.

02

Reducing Choice Complexity

Instead of presenting eight individual service cards, I organised the offerings into three main categories. This creates a simpler starting point for visitors exploring what Nilabery offers.

The Curated Gallery Page

Used colour overlays to integrate the posters into Nilabery’s visual identity, revealing the original artwork on hover.

03

Law of Common Region — Films

Grouped films under distinct category headings to help visitors understand the different types of work.

I used the remaining space to invite people to pitch their stories, creating an opportunity for Nilabery to connect with potential collaborators and showcase new work.

The Recognition Page

Added an animation inspired by my design taste to bring more personality to the section.

04

Law of Common Region

Used separate cards to distinguish individual recognitions.

05

Law of Proximity

Grouped related details, such as the award, festival, and film title, together.

The Teams Page

Kept the two-card layout while turning role descriptions into colourful labels. This makes each person’s role easier to spot while giving the section more of Nilabery’s visual personality.

The About Page

Separated the About and FAQ sections, removing repetitive content and refocusing the FAQs on customers’ most common questions, based on input from the brand.

06

Chunking

Organising information into distinct sections helps visitors process it in smaller, more manageable groups.

The Contact Page

Refocused the section around the main call to action, clarified contact options with icons, and rebalanced the colours and berry graphics to create a more cohesive composition.

The Final Interaction

I believed the interaction should feel intuitive and playful, allowing visitors to discover it naturally while aligning with Nilabery’s brand personality.

Goal: To immerse visitors in Nilabery’s brand identity through an intentional, subtle, and playful interaction that makes exploring the UI an experience in itself.

The Footer

Removed the repeated navigation links because the main navigation was already available earlier on the page. This gave the interactive berry graphics more room and kept the footer focused on a playful brand experience.

The Conclusion

A Few Things I Realised

Through this project, I explored how far AI can take a design, and how much thought it takes to make it feel right. Building Nilabery’s website in a week, instead of the estimated 20–25 days, saved me considerable time. But the process wasn’t a straight path. It involved plenty of back-and-forth iterations, questioning what worked, revisiting decisions, and refining the design at every step.

Using AI didn’t mean stepping away from the design process. If anything, it made it more important to pause, reflect, and validate each decision. AI helped me move faster, but it was the UX thinking and design judgement behind each choice that made the website feel like Nilabery.
